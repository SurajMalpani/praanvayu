प्राणवायु · the breath of life

You can live weeks without food and days without water. Without air, only minutes.

Air pollution was linked to over 20 lakh deaths in India in 2023. That is about one life every 16 seconds[19].

Today’s air stands to cut 3.5 years off the average Indian’s life, and 8.2 years in Delhi[13].

We take about 20,000 breaths a day. All 140 crore Indians breathe air that fails the WHO health guideline[13].

A day of Delhi’s winter air carries a risk like smoking about five cigarettes[12].

We fight for roti, kapda and makaan, but breath comes before all of them, and it is the one need we cannot put off. Air pollution is India's biggest environmental health risk. It doesn't stop at city, state or even national borders, and it is harming every one of us today, from a newborn to a grandparent. We already know where it comes from and what works to clean it up. What's missing is enough people acting. Every day of delay is paid for in lives, so start today.

Our mission

For clean air. For better life. For better planet.

Praanvayu is a climate and public-health initiative working to reduce air pollution across India. Our goal is to bring the national average daily AQI below 100 by 2030, and below 50 by 2035, so that everyone can breathe clean air. Clean air is not a luxury. It is the first necessity of life, before food, water and shelter, and every Indian has a right to it.

We plan to do this through awareness, community action, and working with local governments. Every year of delay costs lakhs of lives. It starts here, and it starts with you.

Understand air pollution

Learn about the air we breathe

You breathe it 20,000 times a day, so know what's in it. Three short guides: what's in polluted air, where it comes from, and what it does to us. Pick one to start.

What's actually in the air?

"Air pollution" is a mix of tiny particles and gases. India's AQI tracks eight of them. The most dangerous is the one you can't see: PM2.5. Pick a pollutant to learn where it comes from and what it does.

How small is PM2.5?

Drawn to scale. A PM2.5 particle is about 30 times thinner than a human hair, small enough to slip past your nose and throat, deep into the lungs and into the blood[15].

What does a PM2.5 reading mean?

Slide to a 24-hour PM2.5 level to see its AQI category, how far it is above the limits, and a rough cigarette comparison. The cigarette figure is a way to grasp the scale of risk, not an exact equivalence.

India's standards vs the WHO guideline

The WHO's 2021 guidelines[1] are based purely on health evidence. India's 2009 standards[7] are legal limits, set several times higher. Both are in µg/m³.

The WHO also sets interim targets as stepping stones; India's annual PM2.5 standard of 40 µg/m³ is close to its least strict interim target (35). India's standards have not been revised since 2009: a review began in 2020, and in December 2025 a parliamentary committee asked the government to notify updated norms[29].

What causes air pollution?

There is no single villain. Nearly all of it comes from burning things: coal and other fuels in factories and power plants, fuel in engines, wood in chulhas, straw in fields, garbage on streets. Ammonia from farms and dust add more, and about a quarter of India's PM2.5 blows in from beyond its borders. Weather then decides whether it blows away or piles up.

The mix is changing. As LPG has reached most homes, household smoke has dropped from the top source to second place nationally, and industry is now the biggest source inside India[18].

Don't forget indoor air

🍳 Cooking

Frying and high-heat cooking release fine particles even on LPG. Use an exhaust fan or chimney, or open a window.

🪔 Incense, dhoop & coils

Agarbatti, dhoop and mosquito coils produce PM2.5. Use them sparingly and ventilate.

🔥 Chulhas & angithis

Wood, dung and coal smoke indoors can be many times worse than outdoor air, and coal heaters in closed rooms can cause deadly CO poisoning. Worldwide, household smoke was linked to about 29 lakh deaths in 2021, including over 3 lakh children under five[3].

🚬 Tobacco smoke

Second-hand smoke is one of the largest sources of indoor PM2.5. Keep homes and cars smoke-free.

🧴 Sprays & paints

Room fresheners, paints and some cleaners release VOCs. Choose low-VOC products and air out rooms.

🦠 Damp & mould

Leaks and damp walls grow mould that triggers asthma. Fix leaks and keep rooms dry.

What dirty air does to the body

Air pollution doesn't only cause coughs. Fine particles inflame the whole body, and the biggest toll is from heart attacks and strokes. This is not a future risk: the damage builds with every polluted breath, even on days you feel fine. Click a numbered spot on the body to see the evidence.

Who is most at risk?

🚨 When to get medical help

  • Chest pain or tightness, or a racing or irregular heartbeat
  • Breathlessness or wheezing that doesn't ease with your usual inhaler
  • A child breathing fast, with ribs pulling in, or too breathless to feed or talk
  • Headache, dizziness or confusion after using a heater or chulha in a closed room (possible CO poisoning: get to fresh air first)

This guide is educational and not a substitute for medical advice.

Practical steps for every AQI

Protect yourself and your family

You can't stop breathing, and you can't wait for the air to get better. But you can cut how much pollution you breathe in by a lot, starting today: check the AQI, time your outdoor activity, clean the air indoors, and wear the right mask when it's bad.

Your action plan for today's AQI

Set today's AQI from the CPCB or SAFAR apps and choose who you're planning for.

😷 Which mask actually works?

MaskAgainst PM2.5
Cloth or scarfLow Fibres and gaps let most fine particles through.
SurgicalLow–medium Better filter, but loose sides leak.
N95 / FFP2High Filters ≥ 95% (N95) or ≥ 94% (FFP2) of fine particles when it seals.
N99 / FFP3Very high Filters more, but is harder to breathe through.

Getting the fit right

  • Use masks with two head straps and a metal nose clip; pinch the clip to your nose.
  • Breathe out hard: if air leaks around the edges, adjust it. Beards break the seal.
  • Replace when it's soiled, damaged or hard to breathe through.
  • Masks aren't safe for babies under 2. Protect young children by limiting exposure and cleaning indoor air.

🏠 Clean the air indoors

  • Close up on bad days. Keep doors and windows shut when AQI is Poor or worse; air out briefly in the cleanest hours.
  • Run a HEPA purifier in the room you spend the most time in, usually the bedroom.
  • Don't add to it. No indoor burning, smoking, incense or candles on smoggy days.
  • Ventilate the kitchen with a chimney or exhaust fan while cooking.
  • Wet-mop and damp-dust instead of sweeping, which kicks particles back up.
  • DIY option: a MERV-13 filter taped to a box fan is a proven low-cost cleaner.

4 · In your community

🏘️ Make your colony burn-free

Get your RWA to ban garbage and leaf burning, set up composting pits, and give guards and gardeners a number to call.

🏗️ Hold builders to dust rules

Construction sites must cover materials, sprinkle water and screen the site. Ask for it, photograph violations and report them.

🚌 Organise car-pools & walking buses

Shared school runs and office car-pools cut traffic and exposure. Push for safe footpaths and cycle lanes.

🍲 Clean kitchens next door

Help domestic workers, guards and nearby families access LPG through Ujjwala, and ventilate kitchens.

🌳 Green with care

Plant native trees and protect existing ones. Trees help locally, but they can't replace cutting emissions.

🎉 Celebrate clean

Organise cracker-free community Diwali, weddings and New Year with lights, music and food.

5 · Report pollution and speak up

  • SAMEER app (CPCB): check hourly AQI and file complaints about garbage burning, dust, industrial smoke and more.
  • Swachhata app (MoHUA): report garbage dumps and burning to your municipality.
  • State Pollution Control Board: every state board takes complaints about factories, kilns and generators.
  • Delhi-NCR: CAQM and the Green Delhi app handle complaints during GRAP periods[9].
  • Write to your MLA, MP and ward councillor. Ask what your city is doing under NCAP[8] and where the money went.
  • Read your city's clean air plan on the PRANA portal, and ask for progress reports.
  • Use RTI to get data on monitoring stations, fines and enforcement.
  • Vote and talk about clean air. When enough people treat it as an election issue, it becomes one.

6 · Find your local officials

Municipal helplines, ward offices and pollution control boards that handle garbage pickup (ghanta gaadi), open burning and dust complaints. Search by pin code or city, anywhere in India.
